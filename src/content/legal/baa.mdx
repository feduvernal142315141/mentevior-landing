This Standard Business Associate Agreement (“BAA”) is entered into between MenteVior LLC, a Florida limited liability company (“MenteVior” or “Business Associate”), and the healthcare provider, agency, practice, business associate, or other organization identified as the customer in an applicable MenteVior order form, subscription agreement, master services agreement, or similar written agreement (“Customer”). Business Associate and Customer are each a “Party” and together are the “Parties.” Customer may be a “Covered Entity” or “Business Associate” as those terms are defined under HIPAA. If Customer is a Business Associate, MenteVior acts as Customer’s subcontractor with respect to PHI governed by this BAA.

## How This BAA Becomes Effective

<LegalCallout title="Publishing this page does not create a business associate relationship">
Posting this BAA on MenteVior’s website does not, by itself, create a business associate relationship.
</LegalCallout>

This BAA becomes effective when Customer accepts or signs an order form, subscription agreement, master services agreement, or other written agreement that expressly incorporates this BAA (the “Underlying Agreement”). The effective date of this BAA is the effective date stated in the Underlying Agreement or, if the Underlying Agreement does not state a separate BAA effective date, the date on which Business Associate first creates, receives, maintains, or transmits Protected Health Information on behalf of Customer.

This BAA supplements and is incorporated into the Underlying Agreement. If the documents conflict regarding the use, disclosure, safeguarding, return, or destruction of Protected Health Information, this BAA controls. The version of this BAA identified in or in effect when the Underlying Agreement is accepted applies unless the Parties later agree in writing to a replacement or amendment. A later website revision does not amend an existing BAA unless the Underlying Agreement expressly provides otherwise or the Parties agree in writing.

## Purpose and Scope

Customer uses MenteVior’s electronic health record, practice-management, hosting, support, backup, data-storage, and related platform services described in the Underlying Agreement (collectively, the “Services”). In performing the Services, Business Associate may create, receive, maintain, or transmit Protected Health Information on behalf of Customer.

The Parties enter into this BAA to comply with the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act of 1996, the Health Information Technology for Economic and Clinical Health Act, and their implementing regulations, each as amended (collectively, “HIPAA”).

## Definitions

Terms not separately defined in this BAA have the meanings assigned to them under HIPAA, including 45 C.F.R. Parts 160 and 164.

For purposes of this BAA:

- “Breach” has the meaning stated in 45 C.F.R. § 164.402.
- “Designated Record Set” has the meaning stated in 45 C.F.R. § 164.501.
- “Electronic Protected Health Information” or “ePHI” has the meaning stated in 45 C.F.R. § 160.103.
- “Protected Health Information” or “PHI” has the meaning stated in 45 C.F.R. § 160.103 and is limited to PHI that Business Associate creates, receives, maintains, or transmits for Customer.
- “Required by Law” has the meaning stated in 45 C.F.R. § 164.103.
- “Secretary” means the Secretary of the United States Department of Health and Human Services or the Secretary’s designee.
- “Security Incident” has the meaning stated in 45 C.F.R. § 164.304.
- “Subcontractor” means a person or entity to whom Business Associate delegates a function, activity, or service involving PHI.
- “Unsecured PHI” has the meaning stated in 45 C.F.R. § 164.402.

## Authorized Uses and Disclosures

Business Associate may use or disclose PHI only:

- to provide, operate, maintain, host, secure, support, troubleshoot, back up, restore, and improve the Services for Customer;
- as directed by Customer through the Services;
- as expressly permitted by this BAA or the Underlying Agreement, provided that the use or disclosure would not violate HIPAA if performed by Customer in its capacity as a Covered Entity or Business Associate; or
- as Required by Law.

Business Associate shall limit its uses, disclosures, and requests for PHI to the minimum necessary to accomplish the intended purpose when the HIPAA minimum-necessary standard applies.

Business Associate may use PHI for its proper management and administration or to carry out its legal responsibilities. It may disclose PHI for those purposes only when the disclosure is Required by Law or after obtaining reasonable assurances from the recipient that:

- the recipient will keep the PHI confidential;
- the recipient will use or further disclose the PHI only as Required by Law or for the purpose for which Business Associate disclosed it; and
- the recipient will notify Business Associate of any known breach of the confidentiality of the PHI.

Business Associate may provide data-aggregation services relating to Customer’s healthcare operations only with Customer’s express written authorization.

Business Associate may use PHI to report violations of law to appropriate federal or state authorities to the extent permitted by 45 C.F.R. § 164.502(j) and other applicable law.

This BAA does not authorize Business Associate to:

- sell PHI;
- use PHI for advertising, marketing, fundraising, or data monetization;
- use PHI to develop or train a general-purpose artificial intelligence or machine-learning model;
- re-identify information that has been de-identified; or
- independently de-identify or use de-identified information.

Any de-identification, use of de-identified information, or additional data use must be authorized in a separate written agreement that states the permitted purpose and the applicable HIPAA standard.

## Business Associate Responsibilities

Business Associate shall:

- not use or disclose PHI except as permitted by this BAA or as Required by Law;
- implement appropriate administrative, physical, and technical safeguards to prevent uses or disclosures not permitted by this BAA;
- comply with the applicable requirements of the HIPAA Security Rule with respect to ePHI;
- mitigate, to the extent practicable, harmful effects known to Business Associate from a use or disclosure of PHI that violates this BAA;
- ensure that workforce members with access to PHI receive appropriate privacy and security training and are subject to confidentiality obligations;
- maintain role-based and least-privilege access, unique user identification, appropriate authentication controls, encryption of ePHI in transit and at rest using industry-standard methods, audit logging, monitoring, vulnerability and patch management, secure-disposal processes, incident-response procedures, and business-continuity procedures;
- make PHI and related information available as required to support Customer’s obligations concerning access, amendment, and accounting of disclosures;
- make applicable internal practices, books, and records available to the Secretary for purposes of determining compliance with HIPAA; and
- comply with the Privacy Rule requirements applicable to Customer when Business Associate performs a Privacy Rule obligation on Customer’s behalf.

## Security Incidents and Breach Reporting

Business Associate shall notify Customer of any use or disclosure of PHI not permitted by this BAA of which Business Associate becomes aware, including a Breach of Unsecured PHI.

Business Associate shall provide initial notice without unreasonable delay and **no later than seventy-two hours** after discovering:

- a Breach of Unsecured PHI;
- a Security Incident involving PHI; or
- another material unauthorized use or disclosure of PHI.

The initial notice shall include, to the extent known at that time:

- a description of the event and the date or estimated date of occurrence and discovery;
- the systems and types of PHI affected;
- the approximate number of affected individuals, if known;
- containment or mitigation measures taken or planned; and
- a contact for follow-up.

Business Associate shall promptly investigate the event, take reasonable steps to contain and mitigate harm, preserve relevant evidence, provide supplemental information as it becomes available, and reasonably cooperate with Customer’s risk assessment, documentation, and notification obligations.

Routine, unsuccessful attempts to access, use, disclose, modify, or destroy information or interfere with system operations do not require individual notice under this section when they do not result in unauthorized access, use, disclosure, modification, or destruction of PHI or material interference with system operations. Examples may include unsuccessful pings, scans, login attempts, denial-of-service attempts, or blocked malware.

## Subcontractors

Before allowing a Subcontractor to create, receive, maintain, or transmit PHI, Business Associate shall require the Subcontractor in writing to comply with the same restrictions, conditions, and requirements that apply to Business Associate with respect to that PHI. Business Associate shall require the Subcontractor to comply with applicable HIPAA Security Rule requirements for ePHI and report Security Incidents and Breaches to Business Associate.

Business Associate remains responsible for its Subcontractors to the extent required by applicable law.

## Support for Individual Rights

Business Associate shall make PHI in a Designated Record Set available to Customer as reasonably necessary for Customer to respond to a request under 45 C.F.R. § 164.524.

At Customer’s direction, Business Associate shall make PHI available for amendment and incorporate an amendment in accordance with 45 C.F.R. § 164.526.

Business Associate shall document disclosures and provide information reasonably necessary for Customer to respond to a request for an accounting of disclosures under 45 C.F.R. § 164.528.

Unless the Parties agree otherwise in writing, Business Associate shall provide information requested under this section within ten business days after receiving Customer’s written request, or sooner when reasonably necessary for Customer to meet an applicable legal deadline.

If Business Associate receives a request concerning PHI directly from an individual, Business Associate may refer the individual to Customer and shall not independently fulfill the request unless Customer directs it to do so or applicable law requires a direct response.

## Customer Responsibilities

Customer shall:

- use the Services and disclose PHI to Business Associate in compliance with HIPAA and other applicable law;
- provide only the PHI reasonably necessary for the Services;
- not direct Business Associate to use or disclose PHI in a manner that would violate HIPAA if performed by Customer;
- maintain all notices, authorizations, consents, and legal permissions required for Customer’s collection, use, and disclosure of PHI;
- notify Business Associate of any limitation in Customer’s notice of privacy practices that affects Business Associate’s permitted use or disclosure of PHI;
- notify Business Associate of any change in or revocation of an individual’s authorization that affects Business Associate’s use or disclosure of PHI;
- notify Business Associate of any restriction on the use or disclosure of PHI to which Customer has agreed and that affects Business Associate;
- configure user access, manage authorized users, protect account credentials, and provide accurate and lawful instructions concerning PHI; and
- not submit PHI through support, marketing, or other communication channels that are not designated by MenteVior for secure transmission.

Customer is responsible for the accuracy, completeness, and lawfulness of PHI and instructions it supplies to the Services, except to the extent a problem results from Business Associate’s failure to comply with this BAA or perform the Services.

## Security Information and Assessments

Upon reasonable written request, Business Associate shall provide Customer with reasonable documentation concerning the privacy and security program applicable to the Services, subject to appropriate confidentiality and security protections.

The Parties shall use a reasonable remote review process before considering an onsite assessment. An onsite assessment must:

- be reasonably necessary and proportionate to a material compliance concern;
- be scheduled in advance during normal business hours;
- avoid unreasonable disruption to Business Associate’s operations;
- protect Business Associate’s confidential information and security controls; and
- avoid access to information belonging to other customers.

## Insurance

During the term of this BAA, Business Associate shall maintain cyber/privacy liability insurance that includes network-security, privacy-liability, regulatory-defense, and breach-response coverage, with limits of at least **$1,000,000 per claim and $1,000,000 in the aggregate**, unless the Underlying Agreement requires higher limits.

Business Associate shall provide reasonable evidence of coverage upon Customer’s written request.

## Term and Termination

This BAA remains effective for as long as Business Associate creates, receives, maintains, or transmits PHI for Customer and until the PHI has been returned, destroyed, or protected under the provisions that survive termination.

Customer may terminate this BAA and the affected Services by written notice if Business Associate materially breaches this BAA and does not cure the breach within ten days after receiving written notice. Customer may terminate immediately if:

- the breach cannot reasonably be cured;
- the breach creates a substantial risk to PHI; or
- immediate termination is necessary to comply with applicable law.

If termination is not feasible, Customer shall report the violation to the Secretary when HIPAA requires it.

## PHI Following Termination

After termination of the applicable subscription, Business Associate will make Customer’s data available for export for **sixty days**, unless the Underlying Agreement states otherwise. During that period, Customer may instruct Business Associate to return or securely destroy the PHI.

After the export period, Business Associate may securely destroy PHI and has no obligation to provide extended or archival retention. Customer is responsible for exporting and independently retaining records it is legally or professionally required to keep.

To the extent feasible, Business Associate shall return or securely destroy all PHI received from Customer or created, maintained, or received on Customer’s behalf and retain no copies. If complete return or destruction is infeasible because of encrypted disaster-recovery backups or a legal retention requirement, Business Associate shall:

- explain the basis for infeasibility in writing upon request;
- retain only the PHI for which return or destruction is infeasible;
- continue to protect the retained PHI under this BAA;
- not actively access, use, or disclose the retained PHI except for the reason that made return or destruction infeasible or as Required by Law; and
- securely destroy the retained PHI when return or destruction becomes feasible or retention is no longer required.

The obligations concerning retained PHI survive termination of this BAA.

## Rights in PHI

As between the Parties, Customer retains its rights in PHI supplied to Business Associate and PHI created or received by Business Associate on Customer’s behalf, subject to individual rights and applicable law.

Business Associate receives no independent ownership interest in PHI. Its rights are limited to the uses and disclosures authorized by this BAA, the Underlying Agreement, and applicable law.

This section does not determine ownership of MenteVior’s software, platform, documentation, templates, workflows, tools, configurations, know-how, or other intellectual property. Those rights are governed by the Underlying Agreement.

## General Provisions

This BAA is governed by Florida law to the extent Florida law is not preempted by federal law.

Except to the extent prohibited by applicable law, the limitations of liability, disclaimers, indemnification provisions, and other allocation-of-risk terms in the Underlying Agreement apply to this BAA. Nothing in the Underlying Agreement or this BAA waives a right or obligation that cannot lawfully be waived under HIPAA or the HITECH Act.

No person other than the Parties is an intended beneficiary of this BAA. If a provision is found invalid or unenforceable, it shall be modified to the minimum extent necessary, and the remaining provisions shall continue in effect. A waiver is effective only if in writing and does not waive a later breach.

The Parties shall cooperate in good faith to amend this BAA when reasonably necessary to comply with a change in applicable law. Except as described in the “How This BAA Becomes Effective” section or expressly permitted by the Underlying Agreement, an amendment must be in writing and accepted by both Parties.

Electronic acceptance, electronic signatures, and counterparts are valid to the extent permitted by applicable law. This BAA may be incorporated into the Underlying Agreement by URL or reference, provided that the applicable version is reasonably identifiable and available to Customer.

The provisions that by their nature must continue after termination, including restrictions on PHI, individual-rights assistance, regulatory access, return or destruction, and continuing protection of retained PHI, survive termination.
